# merit blitz: november 21, 2026

## why this campaign

Run [44669582168](https://github.com/beerooyay/hunter/actions/runs/44669582168),
shard 4, produced and independently rechecked this gap:

```text
172180 29.0002 4466958216814001773*6007#/2310-83194
```

The retained `FOUND.tsv`, raw `gaps.tsv`, and `VERIFIED.tsv` agree on the gap
and expression. Its endpoints are 83,194 below and 88,986 above the center.
The starting number has 2,579 digits. The verifier returned `OK` after GMP
probable-prime checks on both ends and a next-prime adjacency check; this is
not a formal primality certificate.

The four-core shard started at 2026-11-20 03:43:39 UTC. The find was logged at
04:04 UTC, about 20 minutes into the search, and its job finished at 04:06:06.
In America/Chicago these are the evening of November 19, not November 20.
It used the existing record-oriented search, the fixed family `6007#/2310`,
sieve limit 1,000,000,000, and half-window 147,356. No merit-29 optimizer or
special neighbor-search mode produced it.

The winning job's cutoff was 0.066109 modeled records per core-hour. The next
job used 0.28645, 4.333 times higher. The following five core-0 jobs tested zero
of 179 scored candidates while their requested batch sizes increased from
20 to 49, 102, 234 and 445. This motivates a less aggressive cutoff experiment.
It does not prove the winning center would be rejected under the new cutoff:
the modeled value and cost themselves change with the continuation policy.

## evidence used for allocation

All 80 retained regular shards from eight runs were included, including the
zero-find shard whose archive has no `FOUND.tsv`. The sample covers 400 runner
hours, 1,513.769 logged search CPU-hours, 126,489 raw observations, and 29,925
completed gaps. Actual merit is raw column 9, counting from zero. Column 10
is the previous record and must never be counted as a new discovery.

| family | runner hours | actual merit >=20 | >=25 | best actual merit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 6007 / 210 | 120 | 34 | 0 | 24.6138 |
| 6007 / 2310 | 120 | 35 | 3 | 29.0002 |
| 8009 / 210 | 80 | 10 | 0 | 23.6661 |
| 8009 / 2310 | 80 | 14 | 2 | 26.4174 |

The two `/2310` families have identical observed rates of merit >=25 per
runner-hour (0.025); their counts are too small to establish a winner.
Shard 4's two extreme finds do not identify a permanently productive shard.
Fresh, disjoint multipliers are more defensible than replaying a lucky range.

Completed gaps are selected by a record-oriented stopping rule. These are
descriptive campaign outcomes, not unbiased random samples of prime gaps.
The eight runs provide very little information about the true rate of 29+
finds, and this campaign makes no promise of another one.

`blitz_plan.py` fixes and interleaves the same mix in each ten-job group.
The hypothesis is that a lower selection threshold will improve high-merit
yield by completing more promising intervals after the learner becomes
aggressive. It can also waste CPU on weaker candidates. Compare the two
policies within each `/2310` family using actual completed-gap tails and
runner/search CPU time. Do not pool the unmatched `/210` baseline into the
policy comparison. The sieve, record mask, endpoint tests, and independent
verifier are unchanged.

The half-cutoff lane multiplies the ordinary computed lambda by 0.5 for
ordinary search jobs. Dry pricing is unchanged. The bounded cold-start sample
still has lambda zero. `jobs.tsv` records the effective cutoff;
`job-reservations.tsv` additionally records the original learned cutoff at
successful job launch. Status `effective_lambda` describes the next ordinary
job; each live worker's `lambda_used` is the actual cutoff it received.

## reproducibility and result handling

- One build-and-validation job compiles pinned Praetor
  `e51ddcc577bbb2030c75c0b3bbb2ac2ce85c47cd`, runs the safety gate, downloads a
  fresh live record table, and merges 43 independently verified local results.
  The 43 include the latest submitted 30 and 13 recovered historical finds.
- All hunters receive the same tested binary and record snapshot. Their
  hashes, source commit, family, policy and exact integer range are retained.
- A fresh workflow ID reserves a block of 100,000,000 raw multipliers.
  Shard `s` starts at `run_id*100000000 + s*262144 + 1` and has an exclusive
  upper bound 262,144 above its start. The native scheduler enforces that end
  before launching dry, cold, or ordinary jobs. All 256 supported shard slots
  fit in one run block. Reruns are rejected; launch a fresh workflow.
- Search is bounded to five hours, followed by at most five minutes for
  current search jobs and ten minutes for outstanding verification. Remaining
  candidates stay explicitly pending. Cleanup targets only owned process
  groups, not unrelated hunters on the host.
- Every completed actual merit >=20 is exported to `HIGH_MERIT.tsv`, even when
  it does not improve the comparison table and never appears in `FOUND.tsv`.
- `READY.txt` contains independently verified local table improvements;
  `PENDING.tsv` and `FAILED.tsv` keep incomplete or conflicting checks visible.
  Verification identity includes both the full expression and gap size.
  Final submission still requires comparison with the current public table.
- Per-shard raw evidence and the aggregate are retained for 90 days. The
  collector deduplicates candidates while preserving origin and verification
  evidence. No submission to the public gap database is automated.

`job-reservations.tsv` has eleven tab-separated columns:

```text
epoch core arm m0 M dry cold lambda_percent learned_lambda_per_core_hour effective_lambda_per_core_hour end_exclusive
```

## capacity and operational controls

This is one bounded campaign of 100 jobs, each with 18,000 search seconds:
500 runner-hours of search. On the observed four-core standard runners the
planned search capacity is about 2,000 core-hours, before overhead. It is not
a claim that 100 machines run simultaneously. GitHub queues work beyond the
account's actual concurrent-job capacity; no smaller workflow cap is imposed.
The repository's exact account limit was not visible through the available
authenticated connector, and the settings browser was not signed in.

The workflow uses standard public-repository Linux runners. GitHub documents
standard concurrent-job limits of 20 for Free and 40 for Pro personal plans,
and free standard-runner compute in public repositories. No larger paid
runners, plan upgrades, or quota changes are configured. Artifact storage
remains subject to the account's ordinary allowance.

- [GitHub Actions limits](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/limits)
- [GitHub Actions billing](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/concepts/product-billing/github-actions)

The workflow has a single campaign concurrency group and does not cancel an
active blitz when another trigger arrives. To stop a campaign, cancel its
workflow from the Actions page. Fresh launches use the workflow's manual
trigger when available, or a deliberate commit to its trigger file on this
branch. Production and experimental branches from earlier campaigns remain
separate.
