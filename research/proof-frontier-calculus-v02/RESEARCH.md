## theorem 1: exact two-pattern bridge phase criterion

For finite patterns `A,B` and prime `p`, let `M_A = F_p \ {a mod p : a in A}` and similarly `M_B`. Let `a=max(A)`, and join at bridge `g>0` using `A union (a+g+B)`. Then this join omits a residue mod `p` **if and only if**

```
g mod p ∈ (M_A - M_B - a) mod p.
```

Proof: the union omits residue `x` precisely when `x∈M_A` and `x-(a+g)∈M_B`. Writing the second value as `y` gives `g=x-y-a`. Conversely every such pair supplies an omitted residue. The local valid-shift set is a difference set of missing residues, not an empirical guess.

If the total number of offset positions is `N`, primes `p>N` cannot obstruct. Let `Q=∏_{p≤N}p` (prime product). By coordinate-wise CRT, the exact number of admissible bridge classes modulo `Q` is `∏_{p≤N}|G_p|` where `G_p = M_A-M_B-max(A)`. Since admissible sources have nonempty missing sets at every p, at least one allowed phase exists, and a positive bridge no longer than Q exists. Cauchy–Davenport yields the elementary lower bound `|G_p|≥min(p,|M_A|+|M_B|-1)`. The theorem's constituents are classical. The algorithmic interface and certificate are this project's contribution, not a claimed newly published theorem.

The source word alphabet throughout the exhaustive experiment is **{2,4,6,8,10}**. Results are about prime-tuple **admissibility**, not existence of actual simultaneous or consecutive primes.

## theorem 2: exact multi-repair feasibility and counting

For `r` admissible source offset patterns of total cardinality `N`, let `g_1,...,g_{r-1}` be bridging gaps. For each prime `p≤N`, form the finite local feasibility set `T_p⊆(Z/pZ)^(r−1)` of bridge vectors whose entire assembled tuple omits a residue modulo `p`.

Then the combined tuple is admissible **if and only if** the vector reduces into every `T_p`. By coordinate-wise CRT, among bridge vectors modulo `Q` there are **exactly**

```
∏_{p≤N} |T_p|
```

valid residue classes. In particular every collection of individually admissible source patterns is jointly bridgeable: choose a missing residue of each source at each modulus and align the missing residues by choosing block-start translations. CRT aligns those local translations, and adding multiples of Q makes every bridge positive. Each bridge can be chosen in `1..Q`. This is a constructive consequence of elementary finite group arithmetic and CRT, not a guaranteed prime occurrence.

## theorem 3: greedy repair does not optimize total bridge cost

For three admissible source motifs A,B,C, a greedy method first minimizes bridge g for A+B, then minimizes h for (A+B)+C. It need not minimize g+h jointly. A completely explicit counterexample is:

- source gap words `(2,4), (2,4), (4,6)`
- greedy bridges `(18,26)`: total cost **44**
- jointly optimal bridges `(24,2)`: total cost **26**.

The full 9-position patterns are checked against every obstructing prime p≤9. Every positive even candidate with smaller total gap length is exhausted by `path_receipt`, so the counterexample and repaired minimum are finite exact mathematical claims.

The independent exhaustive C study reports:

| source word length | admissible motifs | ordered triples | strict global improvements | mean greedy total | mean global total | worst regret |

| 2 | 17 | 4,913 | 723 | 16.086302 | 14.656625 | 42 |
| 3 | 53 | 148,877 | 35,337 | 31.687688 | 26.770623 | 126 |

The exact optimal finite search uses the greedy construction as a feasible upper bound; it enumerates all positive even bridge pairs with smaller total length, checking primes ≤ total offset cardinality. The local-to-global feasibility theorem makes the search finite and certificate-friendly.

## why this could be useful beyond prime gaps

The second result demonstrates a measurable failure mode for **local mathematical repair**. A future proof-frontier planner should represent multiple repair routes and choose by a global objective under explicit proof obligations. The present result does not establish efficiency on theorem-proving corpora, formal Lean kernel validity, or an ML advantage. Those need separate experiments.
